Data & design notes

Who is this for?

Readers exploring Malaysia's reserve geography: environmental communicators, students and policy-curious audiences. The views support comparison and explanation, not site-level planning.

Why these chart types?

Lines show change over correctly spaced years. Stacked areas reveal composition. Maps answer where; ranked bars support precise comparisons. The paired-share plot compares two measures on one scale. Heatmaps expose repeated state-year patterns; proportional symbols localise frequency.

How is attention guided?

Four chapters move from overview to comparison to context. Forest values use green, population uses purple, flood records use blue, and decreases use rust. Direct annotations identify endpoints and contrasts. Neutral backgrounds and lighter secondary marks keep attention on the evidence.

What should not be inferred?

Recorded reserve change is not equivalent to deforestation. Pre-2017 Peninsular figures include proposed reserves; later ones do not. Flood coverage is selective. Comparisons across dates and datasets are descriptive, not causal.
